# BM25
**In one sentence.** BM25 ("Best Matching 25", Okapi) scores a document for a query by
summing, over query terms, an idf weight times a **saturating** function of term
frequency that is normalized for document length — the strongest simple lexical ranker
and the default in Lucene/Elasticsearch and the [[oasis-search-engine]].

## Why it matters
BM25 has been the hard-to-beat baseline in IR for 30 years; even modern neural systems
usually retrieve BM25 candidates first, then re-rank. It fixes tf-idf's two biggest
flaws — unbounded term frequency and poor length handling — with two interpretable
parameters. Understanding it explains why search results rank the way they do.

## How it works
For a query `Q` and document `D` of length `|D|` with average length `avgdl`:

```
BM25(Q, D) = Σ_{t∈Q}  idf(t) ·  ( f(t,D) · (k1 + 1) )
                                 ───────────────────────────────────
                                 f(t,D) + k1 · (1 − b + b · |D|/avgdl)
```

- `f(t,D)` = term frequency of `t` in `D`.
- `idf(t) = log( (N − df + 0.5) / (df + 0.5) + 1 )` — a smoothed inverse document
  frequency (rare terms weigh more), the probabilistic form.
- **`k1` (≈1.2–2.0)** controls **term-frequency saturation**: as `f` grows, the tf
  factor asymptotes to `k1+1`, so the 10th occurrence of a word adds far less than the
  2nd. This is BM25's key advantage over raw tf-idf.
- **`b` (≈0.75)** controls **length normalization**: `b=1` fully normalizes by document
  length, `b=0` ignores length. It penalizes long documents that match by sheer size.

Scoring streams over the [[inverted-index]] exactly like tf-idf, so it inherits the
same efficiency (and WAND pruning).

**BM25F** extends it to multiple weighted **fields** (title, body, anchor) by combining
per-field term frequencies before saturation — used to boost matches in important
fields such as headings (the [[oasis-search-engine]] applies a heading boost in this
spirit).

## Complexity & trade-offs
- Same cost as tf-idf: O(query terms × posting lengths), with block-max WAND for top-k.
- **Purely lexical** — no synonymy/semantics; "heart attack" won't match "myocardial
  infarction". Pair with [[dense-retrieval-and-embeddings]] via
  [[hybrid-search-and-rank-fusion]].
- Two knobs (`k1`, `b`) to tune per corpus; defaults are robust but not optimal.

## Pitfalls & gotchas
- **Tuning on the test set** — `k1`/`b` should be tuned on held-out queries or you
  overfit; see [[evaluation-of-ir-systems]].
- **Very short documents** (titles-only) interact oddly with length normalization;
  consider lowering `b`.
- **idf can go slightly negative** for terms in >half the documents in the classic
  formula; the "+1" smoothed form above avoids negatives.
- **BM25 scores are not comparable across queries** — they are unnormalized sums, so
  don't threshold a raw BM25 score as a relevance cutoff.

## Worked example
Query "neural search". Document A (short, 100 words) mentions "neural" 3× and "search"
2×; document B (2,000 words) mentions each 5×. With `b=0.75`, B's length normalization
divides its tf contributions down, and tf saturation means A's 3 "neural" hits already
capture most of the available weight — so the concise, on-topic A can outrank the long,
diffuse B, which raw tf-idf might have ranked first.

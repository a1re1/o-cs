# Discrete Mathematics: An Open Introduction (Levin, 3rd ed.)

## What it is
Free, interactive HTML text (PreTeXt) used by many discrete-math courses that do not want the MIT
book's size. Chapters: 0 Introduction & preliminaries (sets, functions); 1 Counting (additive/
multiplicative principles, binomial coefficients, combinations & permutations, stars-and-bars,
[[counting-rules]] via bijections, advanced counting with inclusion–exclusion); 2 Sequences
(arithmetic/geometric, polynomial fitting, solving [[recurrences]], induction); 3 Symbolic logic and
proofs ([[propositional-logic]], [[proof-techniques]]); 4 Graph theory (definitions, planar graphs,
coloring, Euler/Hamilton paths, matching — [[graph-theory-basics]]); 5 Additional topics (generating
functions, introduction to number theory).

## What it adds
- The clearest elementary account of *solving linear recurrences by characteristic roots* and of
  generating functions as "formal power series that remember a sequence" — folded into [[recurrences]].
- A "combinatorial proof" culture: prove identities by counting one set two ways rather than by algebra.
- Less CS-flavoured than [[mcs-lehman-leighton-meyer]]; no invariants, no probability.

# Professional practice, ethics & law: sources

## What they are
- **ACM Code of Ethics** (2018): preamble ("computing professionals' actions change the
  world … the public good is always the primary consideration"); §1 general principles,
  §2 professional responsibilities, §3 leadership, §4 compliance; with case studies
  (malware disruption, automated active response weapons, dark UX patterns, medical
  implant devices, abusive workplace behavior, autonomous systems). The **IEEE-CS/ACM
  Software Engineering Code of Ethics** (1999) with its eight principles (public, client
  and employer, product, judgment, management, profession, colleagues, self) is the
  SE-specific companion; the **NSPE code** ("hold paramount the safety, health, and
  welfare of the public") the engineering baseline.
- **Stanford CS181/181W** (Winstein & Gregg; read: Spring 2024 page): goals — recognize
  ("a grasshopper on your shoulder"), reason, persuade; writing assignments (40 %),
  project (30 %), participation; Unit 1 risk and responsibility (Therac-25 memo; Bernstein
  on causal proportion; Hofstadter; Hormio on culpable ignorance; Patrick Racing
  decision exercise; Iran Air 655); later units on privacy and surveillance, speech and
  platforms, fairness and algorithms, IP, AI policy.
- **Harvard Embedded EthiCS** (read: modules index): 100+ modules embedded in CS
  courses, tagged by CS area and ethics topic (design process and evaluation, ethical
  theory, fairness and bias, goods and values, governance, knowledge and information,
  privacy, responsibility and action, resource distribution, rights, safety/
  explainability/transparency); e.g. responsibility in software design; red teaming;
  modules on differential privacy, recommender systems, accessibility, ML fairness.
- **MIT 6.805/6.4590** (Foundations of Information Policy; Abelson, Weitzner): law and
  policy of privacy, encryption, copyright, free speech, with the *Blown to Bits* text.
- **Texts**: O'Neil 2016 (*Weapons of Math Destruction*: opacity, scale, damage — models
  that punish the poor, teacher value-added, recidivism scores, predatory ads, workplace
  scheduling, credit and insurance, the feedback loops that make them self-fulfilling);
  Quinn (10e 2022; the standard survey with ethical-theory toolkit: Kantianism, act/rule
  utilitarianism, social contract, virtue ethics); Weizenbaum 1976 (ELIZA's users
  confiding in a script; the difference between deciding and choosing; "since we do not
  now have any ways of making computers wise, we ought not now to give computers tasks
  that demand wisdom"); Leveson & Turner 1993 (Therac-25: six overdoses 1985–87 from race
  conditions and a reused-software assumption, removed hardware interlocks, cryptic
  error messages, and an organization that didn't believe the reports — the lessons on
  safety culture); Brey, Nissenbaum (contextual integrity), Zuboff (surveillance
  capitalism), Noble (*Algorithms of Oppression*), Benjamin (*Race After Technology*).

## What they add
The codes give the profession's own stated obligations, quotable clause by clause; CS181
and Embedded EthiCS show how the discipline actually teaches this — recognize, reason,
persuade, in writing — rather than as a list of prohibitions; Therac-25 and O'Neil are
the two case literatures (safety-critical failure, and scaled opaque decision systems)
that every engineer should be able to retell.

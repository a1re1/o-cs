# Stanford CS103 Mathematical Foundations of Computing

## What it is
Level 200, the prerequisite for CS154/CS161. Summer 2026 lecture list: 0 set theory & intro to proofs;
1 applications of proofs, negations; 2 propositional logic, existential & universal quantifiers;
3 more propositional logic and functions; 4 functions and graphs; 5 proofs about graphs and the
pigeonhole principle; 6–7 induction; 8 finite automata; 9 more finite automata and regular
expressions; 10 NFAs → regular expressions; 11 CFGs and intro to Turing machines; 12 Turing machines
and R vs RE; 13 decidability and the halting problem; 14 P vs NP and complexity theory. Ships a
"Proofwriting Checklist", a truth-table tool, and seven problem sets.

## Key ideas → pages
- First-order logic as a *precise language* for stating what you are proving: quantifier order,
  negating quantified statements, translating English — [[first-order-logic]].
- The proofwriting checklist (state what you assume, name variables, one idea per paragraph) — the
  same rules as [[proof-techniques]].
- The second half is the on-ramp to §5.1: [[computability-and-halting-problem]] here; automata and
  complexity in [[finite-automata-and-regular-languages]], [[turing-machines]], [[p-vs-np]].

## What it adds
The shortest path from "can write a proof" to "understands undecidability and P vs NP" (10 weeks);
its automata material is the concrete side that [[open-logic-project]] treats abstractly.

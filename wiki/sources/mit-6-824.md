# MIT 6.824 and related courses

## What it is
Spring 2025 sequence: 1 introduction (why distributed — performance, fault tolerance,
physical separation, security; the hard parts — concurrency, partial failure, performance);
2 RPC and threads in Go; 3 GFS; 4 Paxos pseudo-code; 5 Go patterns (Russ Cox); 6–7 fault
tolerance: Raft; 8 consistency and linearizability; 9 ZooKeeper; 11 distributed
transactions (2PL + 2PC); 12 Spanner; 13 chain replication (CRAQ); 14 optimistic concurrency
control (FaRM); 15 verification of distributed systems; 16 cache consistency: Memcached at
Facebook; 17 AWS Lambda (Marc Brooker); 18 Ray; 19 fork consistency, SUNDR; 20 peer-to-peer:
Bitcoin; 21 Byzantine fault tolerance. **Labs (Go)**: 1 MapReduce (coordinator + workers over
RPC, fault tolerance by reassignment); 2 key/value server (linearizable put/append with
at-most-once RPC); 3 Raft — A leader election, B log replication, C persistence, D log
compaction/snapshots; 4 fault-tolerant KV service on Raft (client retries, duplicate
detection); 5 sharded KV with a shard controller and live reconfiguration.
**Kleppmann, Cambridge Distributed Systems** (notes + YouTube): introduction; models of
distributed systems (network/node/timing assumptions, fault detectors); time and clocks
(physical clock sync, Lamport/vector clocks); broadcast protocols and logical time (FIFO/
causal/total-order broadcast); replication (quorums, state-machine replication); consensus
(Raft); consistency models (linearizability, eventual consistency, CRDTs); case studies
(Google Spanner, collaborative editing). **CS244B**: paper reading + project. **15-440/640**:
systems programming, RPC, DFS, consistency, fault tolerance, big data.

## What it adds
The Raft lab is the rite of passage: nothing teaches partial failure like a test harness that
drops, delays, and reorders your RPCs. Kleppmann's notes are the fastest correct theory.

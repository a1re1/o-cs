# MIT 6.042J Mathematics for Computer Science (OCW, Spring 2015)

## What it is
Level-200 required math course for MIT CS majors; the OCW Spring 2015 offering (Albert Meyer) has
lecture videos, class problems with solutions, problem sets, and the full text
[[mcs-lehman-leighton-meyer]]. Course units mirror the book's four parts. Prerequisite for 6.006 /
6.046 algorithms. Renumbered 6.1200 in 2022.

## How it is taught
Flipped: read the text, then in-class team problem solving. Three "units": Proofs, Structures,
Counting/Probability. Assessment emphasises writing clear proofs (see [[proof-techniques]]).

## Key ideas (as sequenced)
1. Proofs → well ordering → logic → sets/relations → induction & state machines ([[induction]], [[invariant-principle]]).
2. Number theory & RSA ([[number-theory-basics]]); directed graphs, DAGs, partial orders
   ([[dags-and-partial-orders]]); simple graphs, matching, coloring ([[graph-theory-basics]]).
3. Sums & asymptotics ([[asymptotic-notation]]); counting ([[counting-rules]], [[pigeonhole-principle]]);
   generating functions ([[recurrences]]).
4. Probability: [[four-step-method]], conditional probability, random variables, expectation, deviation
   from the mean ([[concentration-inequalities]]), random walks.

## Related

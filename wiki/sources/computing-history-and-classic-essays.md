# Computing History & Foundational Essays (Various)

## What it is
The self-reflective literature of computing: how the field's ideas and institutions came
to be, and the essays where its practitioners distilled hard-won wisdom about building
software and doing research. Reading it gives perspective that no technical text does.

## Key ideas
- **The great essays** — recurring lessons on complexity, simplicity, and taste. See
  [[classic-essays-of-computing]].
- **Turing Award lectures** — each laureate's synthesis of a subfield (free from ACM).
- **Narrative histories** — how machines, labs, and cultures actually formed.

## Chapter / lecture map
- **Lewis (ed.), *Ideas That Created the Future*** — ~46 annotated classic papers
  (Turing, Shannon, von Neumann, Dijkstra, Codd, Cook, and more).
- **ACM Turing Award lectures (free)** — e.g. Dijkstra "The Humble Programmer," Hoare
  "The Emperor's Old Clothes," Backus "Can Programming Be Liberated…".
- **Waldrop, *The Dream Machine*** — Licklider and the origins of personal/interactive
  computing. **Hafner & Lyon, *Where Wizards Stay Up Late*** — the ARPANET.
- **Levy, *Hackers*; Kidder, *The Soul of a New Machine*** — the culture and grind of
  building.
- **Computer History Museum oral histories (free).**

## Notable claims & quotes (the essays)
- **Brooks, "No Silver Bullet" (1986)** — no single advance will give an order-of
  -magnitude gain, because software's *essential* complexity (not accidental) is the hard
  part. See [[classic-essays-of-computing]].
- **Dijkstra, "Go To Statement Considered Harmful" (1968)** — structured control flow;
  and "The Humble Programmer."
- **Hamming, "You and Your Research" (1986)** — how to do work that matters.
- **Gabriel, "Worse Is Better" (1991)** — simpler, shippable designs often beat "the
  right thing."
- **Hoare, "The Emperor's Old Clothes" (1980)** — simplicity and the perils of ambition
  (the null reference as his "billion-dollar mistake").

## What it adds
Perspective, taste, and the "why" behind conventions the rest of the wiki treats as
given. Connects to [[software-engineering-fundamentals]] (Brooks), [[modularity-and-information-hiding]]
(Parnas), and [[computing-ethics-and-professional-responsibility]].
